## 本期导读与来源

## 一、英文阅读

### Why Keep Your First Guess?

Imagine that a friend shows you a jar of buttons and asks how many are inside. You write down 38. A moment later, you notice how much of the jar is hidden behind the label and raise your estimate to 56. Your friend counts out 46 buttons. The second guess was worse than the first, yet the number halfway between them, 47, missed the answer by just one. Could something similar happen often enough to make a second guess worth keeping?

To find out, a team in Belgium returned to an earlier experiment. Students answered eight questions that called for a percentage. One asked them to estimate the share of the world’s airports located in the United States. They had to rely on what they already knew. One group gave a different answer to each question as soon as the first round was over. Another group came back three weeks later. For each student and each question, the researchers could now compare the first guess, the second guess and their average with the known answer.

The second round might sound like a chance to correct mistakes. In fact, when the researchers scored the second guesses on their own, the students did worse overall in both groups. Yet when the researchers averaged each pair of guesses before scoring it, the errors were smaller overall than when either set of guesses was scored alone. If a student had crossed out the first answer and handed in the second, that student would have lost the very information needed to take the average.

It helps to picture the errors as having a direction. With the buttons, one guess fell short and the other went too far, so the two errors partly canceled each other out. Now imagine that you had guessed 55 and 57. Their average, 56, would still be ten buttons too high. Two answers from the same person can carry the same mistake, especially when both depend on a detail the person has remembered incorrectly.

Three weeks might seem long enough to give anyone a fresh view. Both groups did gain from averaging, but the study found no clear evidence that waiting three weeks brought a larger gain over the first guess. Next time you have to estimate a number you cannot easily check, try keeping your first answer on the page. When you take a second look, you can ask what the two numbers tell you together.

## 二、篇章解读

**主旨与态度**　第二次估计即使单独得分较差，也可能提供第一次估计所缺少的信息。作者肯定把两个数保留下来取平均的做法，同时用 *overall* 限定结论的范围，用 *no clear evidence* 保留对等待时间的判断。

| 段落 | 读到哪一句时，论证发生了变化？ | 这一段解决的问题 |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *yet the number halfway between them …* | 第二次猜 56，比第一次猜 38 偏得更远，两数的中点 47 却只差 1。由一个能算清楚的例子引出问题，这种好处是否常见？ |
| 2 | *One group … Another group …* | 两组都对八道百分比题估计两次，作答间隔不同。比较时，先将同一个人对同一道题的两次答案配对。 |
| 3 | *on their own* 与 *averaged each pair* | 第二轮单独评分比第一轮差。改用每对答案的平均值评分，整体误差却低于单用第一轮或第二轮。比较对象换了，两句话可以同时成立。 |
| 4 | *Now imagine … 55 and 57.* | 38 和 56 分居真值两边，偏差能部分抵消。55 和 57 都偏高，中点也偏高。这个对照说明取平均为何可能有用，以及它何时仍会留下较大误差。 |
| 5 | *a larger gain over the first guess* | 两组都能从取平均中受益。若把第一次估计作为基准，这次研究没有给出明确证据，证明多等三周还能扩大收益。 |

**把证据读准**　研究用均方误差衡量准确度。先求估计值与真值之差，将差平方，再取平均，数值越小越好。原论文表 4 中，即时组第一猜、第二猜、两猜均值的得分依次为 589、615、541，延迟组依次为 517、589、467。以第一猜为基准，两组分别改善 48 和 50。仅凭 50 大于 48，不能断定等待三周更有效。要判断等待的作用，比较的是两组各自**改善了多少**。

## 三、长难句精读

**1. 让步与结果**

> The second guess was worse than the first, yet the number halfway between them, 47, missed the answer by just one.

*yet* 连接两个完整分句。后半句的主干是 *the number … missed the answer*。*halfway between them* 修饰 *number*，*47* 是同位语。*by just one* 表示差了多少。

**直译**　第二次猜得比第一次偏得更远，但位于两个猜测值正中间的数 47，和答案只差 1。

**2. 先配对，再评分**

> Yet when the researchers averaged each pair of guesses before scoring it, the errors were smaller overall than when either set of guesses was scored alone.

主干是 *the errors were smaller overall*。第一个 *when* 从句交代误差较小的情形，*before* 点明先后顺序。*it* 指每一对猜测取平均后得到的结果。*than when …* 引出另一种评分情形，*either set* 指第一轮或第二轮的整组答案。

**直译**　然而，当研究者先将每对猜测取平均、再给这个结果评分时，整体误差比单独给任一轮猜测评分时更小。

**3. 找到比较的基准**

> Both groups did gain from averaging, but the study found no clear evidence that waiting three weeks brought a larger gain over the first guess.

第一个 *did* 用来强调，后接动词原形 *gain*。后一个 *gain* 是名词。*that* 引出的从句说明 *evidence* 的具体内容。*over the first guess* 给出衡量收益的基准，*larger* 则比较延迟组和即时组的收益大小。

**直译**　两组确实都从取平均中获益，但以第一次猜测为基准，研究没有找到明确证据表明，等待三周会带来更大的改善。

## 五、读后检查

**1. Which claim is best supported by the study?**

A. Giving a second answer generally improved the students’ scores when only that answer was marked.

B. Combining each student’s two guesses reduced error overall in both groups.

C. Every student benefited more from waiting three weeks than from answering again immediately.

**答案 B。** 第 3 段的 *each pair* 说明配对发生在同一人、同一题内，*overall* 表明结论来自汇总结果。A 将“取平均的收益”换成了“第二次回答的收益”；C 把整体结果扩大到每个人，还额外断定了等待的优势。

**2. 第 4 段为什么又举 55 和 57 这组数？**　两数都高于 46，平均数 56 仍偏高。这个例子说明，即使总体有收益，面对同一个被记错的事实，再猜一次仍可能沿着原来的方向出错。

**3. 用一句英文概括实验发现，并保留结论的范围。**　Across the questions, students made smaller errors when their two guesses were averaged, even though their second guesses alone were less accurate.
